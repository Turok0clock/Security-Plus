P|Governance assigns authority, accountability, and requirements. A policy states what the organization requires and why; a standard supplies mandatory measurable details; a procedure gives steps; a guideline offers recommended practice. The same subject can appear in all four documents at different levels. Do not classify a document by how important its topic sounds; classify its function and whether it is mandatory.
P|Data roles separate decisions from implementation. Owners approve classification and access requirements; custodians implement handling and access controls. Controllers determine purposes and means of personal-data processing; processors act on the controller's behalf under the applicable arrangement. A data subject is the person the information concerns. Governance also covers policy lifecycle, exceptions, independent oversight, and secure development processes. An acceptable-use policy governs user behavior, not the software development lifecycle.
F|Define requirement|Assign accountable owner|Specify standards and procedures|Implement and review|Manage exceptions and updates
T|Policy|A high-level mandatory statement of organizational requirements and direction. It establishes what is required; detailed values and task sequences typically appear elsewhere.
T|Standard|A mandatory specific requirement, such as minimum key length or required logging fields. It translates policy into consistent measurable implementation criteria.
T|Procedure|A step-by-step sequence for carrying out a task. It explains how operators should implement the requirement in a repeatable way.
T|Guideline|A recommended approach that allows discretion when appropriate. It differs from a compulsory policy or standard, though local governance should define the hierarchy.
T|Data owner|Accountable for business classification, access requirements, and handling decisions. The owner approves who should have access rather than necessarily editing every technical permission.
T|Data custodian|Implements and maintains approved data controls, including storage, backups, and access configuration. The role administers the owner's decisions rather than independently setting business purpose.
T|Data controller|Determines purposes and means of personal-data processing in the applicable privacy framework. Controller responsibilities are distinct from merely hosting or operating systems.
T|Data processor|Processes personal data on behalf of a controller according to the arrangement. Its authority is bounded by the controller's instructions and applicable obligations.
T|Governance oversight|Provides accountable direction and independent review of security performance, risk, and policy compliance. It does not replace day-to-day operational administration.
T|Exception process|Documents departures from requirements with rationale, risk approval, compensating controls, an owner, and an expiry or review. Exceptions should not silently become permanent defaults.
T|Development lifecycle policy|Governs secure design, implementation, testing, release, maintenance, and retirement of software. It differs from an acceptable-use policy directed at users' permitted behavior.
T|Acceptable use policy|Defines permitted and prohibited use of organizational resources by users. It can address personal use, prohibited applications, and responsibilities, but is not the application development process.
E|The owner approves read access for the finance team; the custodian configures the access list and maintains backups. A policy requires protected information to be encrypted; a standard specifies approved algorithms; a procedure describes key setup; a guideline suggests convenient practices. Identify the decision level instead of treating all security documents as interchangeable.

P|Risk combines uncertainty and consequences. Identify the asset, threat, weakness, likelihood, impact, existing controls, and owner. A risk register records the risk and treatment over time. Appetite expresses broad willingness to take risk; tolerance defines acceptable variation or thresholds in a particular context. Acceptance should be an authorized informed decision, not simply failure to act.
P|Treatment changes the exposure in different ways: avoid stops the activity, mitigate reduces likelihood or impact, transfer shifts some financial consequences or responsibilities, and accept retains the remaining risk. Insurance does not make an attack impossible. Quantitative analysis separates one-event loss from yearly frequency. Single Loss Expectancy equals asset value times exposure factor in the simple model; Annualized Loss Expectancy equals SLE times Annualized Rate of Occurrence. ARO can be less than one.
F|Identify and own|Estimate likelihood and impact|Choose treatment|Approve residual risk|Monitor and revise
T|Risk register|A maintained record of risks, owners, likelihood, impact, treatment, and status. It supports decisions and follow-up rather than only listing vulnerabilities.
T|Risk appetite|The organization's broad willingness to take risk in pursuit of objectives. It guides decisions across activities rather than serving as one specific service threshold.
T|Risk tolerance|Acceptable variation or boundaries for a specific risk or objective. It translates broad appetite into operational limits that can be monitored.
T|Accept|An authorized party knowingly retains the risk, often because treatment cost or feasibility does not justify further action. Record rationale and review conditions.
T|Avoid|Stops or does not undertake the risk-generating activity. It can remove that exposure while sacrificing the associated business opportunity.
T|Transfer|Shifts some financial consequences or responsibilities through insurance or contracts. Residual operational, reputational, and legal exposure may remain.
T|Mitigate|Reduces likelihood or impact using safeguards such as filtering, training, segmentation, or backups. It does not necessarily eliminate all risk.
T|SLE|Single Loss Expectancy: estimated monetary loss from one occurrence. In the basic asset model, SLE equals asset value multiplied by the exposure factor.
T|ARO|Annualized Rate of Occurrence: expected frequency per year. One event every four years corresponds to 0.25, not four events annually.
T|ALE|Annualized Loss Expectancy: estimated yearly loss, calculated as SLE multiplied by ARO in the simple quantitative model.
T|Residual risk|Risk remaining after controls or treatment. Evaluate and assign ownership rather than assuming that installing a safeguard reduces risk to zero.
E|An outage would cost $40,000 and is expected once every four years. SLE is $40,000, ARO is 0.25, and ALE is $10,000 per year. A control that reduces frequency changes ARO; one that limits loss per event changes SLE. Buying insurance transfers some financial exposure, while eliminating the service avoids that activity's risk.

P|Third-party risk starts before purchase and continues through changes and termination. Due diligence evaluates capabilities, evidence, access, data use, support, and dependencies. A questionnaire is useful but does not prove every answer. Match assurance to risk through independent reports, testing rights, technical validation, and ongoing monitoring. A supplier's subcontractors can extend the trust chain.
P|Agreement names describe different functions. A Service-Level Agreement sets measurable service commitments. A Statement of Work defines specific deliverables and scope. A Master Service Agreement establishes overarching relationship terms. A Non-Disclosure Agreement restricts disclosure. A Memorandum of Understanding records common intent; a Memorandum of Agreement records agreed actions or responsibilities. Actual obligations depend on the document's wording, so do not treat a title as a universal legal rule.
F|Assess before selection|Define scope and responsibilities|Set measurable commitments|Verify throughout service|Revoke and close at exit
T|Due diligence|Evaluates a prospective partner's security, capabilities, support, and risks before relying on it. Evidence quality and scope matter more than a completed checklist alone.
T|Right-to-audit clause|Provides contractual rights to examine or obtain assurance about relevant controls under agreed conditions. It does not itself perform the audit.
T|SLA|Service-Level Agreement specifies measurable commitments such as uptime, response, restoration, and remedies. It differs from the work deliverables themselves.
T|NDA|Non-Disclosure Agreement governs disclosure of protected information. It does not by itself define service performance or authorize technical access.
T|MOU|Memorandum of Understanding records shared intentions or understanding between parties. Its effect depends on the text and applicable context, not the acronym alone.
T|MOA|Memorandum of Agreement documents agreed responsibilities or actions between organizations. Read the actual provisions rather than assuming all such documents have identical force.
T|MSA|Master Service Agreement sets general terms governing an ongoing service relationship. Individual projects may be defined through separate statements of work.
T|SOW|Statement of Work specifies project scope, tasks, deliverables, schedules, and acceptance criteria. It defines what work is to be done.
T|Supply chain assessment|Examines risks arising from suppliers, components, distribution, and dependencies, including downstream or subcontractor exposure. It is broader than one vendor's claimed controls.
T|Vendor monitoring|Reassesses supplier performance and security over time, including changes, incidents, access, and support status. Initial approval is not perpetual assurance.
E|For a camera installation, the SOW defines locations, installation tasks, and acceptance tests. The SLA may define support response times. The MSA establishes general commercial terms, and the NDA limits disclosure of sensitive drawings. A right-to-audit clause may permit control verification. Read the requested outcome to choose the appropriate agreement.
